Local Service in Midvale

Midvale, old streets and a new west side

Midvale became a city in 1909. The name came from a contest, because the town sits in the middle of the Salt Lake Valley. Before that it was called Bingham Junction, a rail stop for the Bingham Canyon mines, and smelters ran along the Jordan River on the west side for a long time. Downtown is the Midvale Main Arts and Culture District, with murals, shops and the old street grid around Holden Street. City Hall is at 7505 S Holden Street, and Midvale City Park is a short walk east of there.

The housing tells the same two-sided story. East Midvale and the Fort Union area Midvale later added still have mid-century ranches and split-levels, plus bungalows near the historic core. The west side is much newer. After a long cleanup of the old Sharon Steel slag ground, the site came off the federal National Priorities List in 2006, and Bingham Junction filled in with townhomes, condos and offices around the Red Line TRAX station. Jordan Bluffs, just south of that, is the next stretch of the same work. The city grew from 27,964 people in 2010 to 36,028 in 2020, and the census counted 15,430 housing units that year.

Leaving Targhee Drive, Midvale City Hall, Main Street and the Bingham Junction TRAX station are about 15 minutes away. The east side near Hillcrest High and the Shops at Fort Union is closer to 20. I-15 cuts through the middle of town, I-215 is the north edge of the east side, and 7800 South runs across Midvale. Midvale also has Midvale Center and Midvale Fort Union stations on the Blue Line. Tell us whether you're by Main Street, Bingham Junction or Fort Union when you call.

Garage door trouble we run into in Midvale

Hardware that has been on the door a long time

Around Main Street and in East Midvale, a lot of doors went on mid-century homes or even earlier bungalows. Those torsion or extension springs and the rollers and cables wear out, and some openers predate the 1993 rule that required safety sensors. If yours is that old, or the sensors are missing, we'll tell you straight and quote a replacement.

The original springs on a newer west side home

Townhomes and condos around Bingham Junction and Jordan Bluffs went up after that 2006 cleanup, so plenty of those doors are now hitting the end of the first spring life. The springs I put on are 15,000 cycle or 25,000 cycle rated. If one spring fails on a two spring setup, I install a matched pair. That keeps the door even.

A cold snap after a hot summer

January lows in Midvale average around 17 degrees, and July highs average about 93. That swing dries out the bottom seal and makes a worn spring more apt to snap on a freezing morning. A pre-winter 15-point inspection looks at the springs, rollers and balance so you are less likely to get stuck.

An opener that hums, or eyes that will not line up

On both the older east side doors and the newer west side ones, the usual opener jobs are worn gears, a failed circuit board or photo eyes that got knocked crooked. We fix what is worth fixing. If the opener is done, I prefer LiftMaster and Chamberlain, and I also install Genie and Craftsman.
