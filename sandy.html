Garage Doors Across Sandy's Neighborhoods

Sandy stretches from State Street and I-15 up to the foot of the Wasatch Range, and its garages change as you climb. Near the Shops at South Town, the Mountain America Expo Center, and America First Field, home of Real Salt Lake, you'll find established neighborhoods where doors have been repaired, replaced, or repainted over the years.

Farther east, toward Dimple Dell Regional Park, Alta Canyon, and the mouth of Little Cottonwood Canyon, homes sit higher on the bench. Those streets often get more snow and colder canyon air than the valley floor, and many have sloped driveways where meltwater runs straight toward the garage door and refreezes overnight.

Sandy grew fastest in the 1970s, 1980s, and 1990s, and many homes date to those decades. Doors from that era are often uninsulated, may lift on extension springs, and are paired with openers that predate modern safety and security features. We're glad to repair what you have, and we'll be straightforward when an upgrade makes more sense.

What Sandy Garage Doors Need Most

Extension springs on older doors

Extension springs stretch along the tracks on each side of the door. When one breaks without a safety cable running through it, it can whip across the garage. If your older door has no safety cables, we can add them or talk through converting to torsion springs.

Sloped driveways and ice

On bench lots, water runs down the driveway and freezes against the door overnight. A fresh bottom seal helps, and a properly adjusted opener won't strain against a frozen seal.

Openers from another era

Very old openers may lack the photo-eye safety sensors required on openers sold since 1993, and the rolling-code security newer remotes use. A current opener adds both, plus options like battery backup and phone control.

Afternoon sun on west-facing doors

Summer afternoons are hot, and dark west-facing doors absorb a lot of that heat. An insulated door keeps the garage, and any room above it, noticeably cooler.

Sandy Garage Door FAQ

Do you service the east bench and Little Cottonwood area?

Yes. We serve all of Sandy, including 84070, 84092, 84093, and 84094, from State Street to the foothills.

Is it worth repairing a 30-year-old garage door?

Often, yes. If the panels are solid, new springs, rollers, and cables can give an older door years more life. If the panels are cracked or rusted through, or you want a warmer garage, replacement may be the better value. We'll give you both options.

How can I tell if my old opener is still safe?

Lay a roll of paper towels flat under the door and close it. If the door doesn't reverse when it touches the roll, or the opener has no photo-eye sensors near the floor, it should be repaired or replaced.

Which doors hold up best to Sandy winters?

Insulated steel doors with good weatherstripping and a tight bottom seal handle snow and cold well. We'll help you choose the insulation level and style that fit your home.
